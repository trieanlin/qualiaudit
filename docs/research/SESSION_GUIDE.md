# Moderated session guide

This guide supports a formative session using the fictional synthetic demo. It
is not a consent script and must be paired with institution-approved participant
information and consent materials.

## Facilitator stance

- Be neutral about QualiAudit and the AI reading.
- Do not call a suggestion correct, accurate, validated, or better.
- Do not imply that changing a human decision is desirable.
- Ask for evidence and reasoning without demanding consensus.
- Accept rejection, preservation of both readings, or intentional
  non-resolution as legitimate outcomes.
- Use the participant’s own method language where it does not conflict with the
  study boundary.
- Record help given and deviations from the task order.

If a participant attempts to import real data, stop the task and return to the
synthetic sample.

## Before the session

Confirm:

- governance approval and current participant consent procedure;
- QualiAudit commit/deployment and protocol version;
- local deterministic reviewer selected;
- no API key or remote reviewer needed;
- synthetic project reset to its initial state;
- requested access accommodations available;
- recording off unless separately approved and consented;
- observation file named with a pseudonymous study ID only.

## Opening

After completing the approved consent procedure:

> Today we are evaluating the workflow, not your qualitative research ability.
> The material is fictional. The AI reading is not an answer key, and keeping,
> rejecting, combining, or leaving interpretations unresolved may all be
> reasonable. You can pause or stop under the procedure described in your study
> information.

Ask:

- “What do you think this tool is for?”
- “What would you be cautious about in an AI-assisted coding review?”
- “Which analytic approach best describes how you want to approach today’s
  synthetic task?”

Do not correct expectations yet unless the participant believes real data or a
verified answer key is involved.

## Task 1 — project and material orientation

Prompt:

> Please open the synthetic review and talk me through the project, research
> question, method, codebook, and existing human coding.

The demo opens on **Review materials**. Ask the participant to open **Project
brief** before inspecting the codebook and excerpts so the method and intended
AI role are considered before the review materials.

Observe whether the participant notices:

- fictional-data labelling;
- the intended role of AI;
- method selection;
- code definitions and inclusion/exclusion guidance;
- human first-pass rationale and confidence;
- optional second-human information as distinct from AI review.

Neutral probes:

- “What information are you using?”
- “What, if anything, is unclear?”

## Task 2 — freeze and blind-review boundary

Before the participant freezes the interpretation, ask:

- “What do you expect will be saved?”
- “What do you think the reviewer will and will not see?”
- “What would you need to verify before continuing?”

After freezing, ask them to run the local reviewer. If they select the remote
option, ask what disclosure they expect, then return to the local reviewer for
this study.

Do not reveal the expected answer before the participant explains their model of
the boundary.

## Task 3 — review queue

Ask the participant to inspect:

1. an overlap case;
2. a divergent or alternative-reading case;
3. a context, confidence, boundary, ambiguity, or unsupported-reading case.

Rotate case order according to the approved assignment.

For each case, ask:

- “What draws your attention first?”
- “What evidence supports your reading?”
- “What would make you resist or use the AI reading?”
- “Is another interpretation still worth preserving?”
- “What information is missing?”

Avoid “Do you agree with the AI?” as the first prompt because it centres the AI
position before the participant’s reasoning.

## Task 4 — human resolution

Prompt:

> Choose the outcome that best represents your current analytic judgment and
> record a short rationale. There is no preferred choice.

After the participant saves:

- “What changed, if anything, after seeing the AI reading?”
- “Which part of your rationale is yours, and which part came from the
  comparison?”
- “Would you want this case to remain unresolved or be discussed with another
  researcher?”

Record whether the participant:

- independently cites excerpt/context/codebook evidence;
- adopts model wording without further support;
- rejects the AI because it is AI rather than because of the material;
- preserves multiple interpretations;
- searches for missing context;
- feels pressured by defaults, labels, visual hierarchy, or queue placement.

These are observations, not correctness judgments.

## Task 5 — method-specific audit action

### Codebook / Framework route

Ask the participant to inspect a possible ambiguity and, if appropriate, propose
a codebook-change record.

Probe:

- “What would make this a codebook issue rather than a case-specific
  disagreement?”
- “Which excerpts might require recoding?”
- “What should remain unchanged in the frozen record?”

### Reflexive Thematic Analysis route

Ask the participant to add a reflexive memo after a decision.

Probe:

- “What did the alternative reading make more visible or complicated?”
- “What assumptions or positioning shaped your reading?”
- “What should remain productively unresolved?”

Do not ask for consensus, accuracy, or coding reliability in this route.

## Task 6 — audit and data boundary

Ask the participant to inspect the decision log and export choices.

Prompts:

- “What could another researcher understand from this audit?”
- “What might they incorrectly infer?”
- “Which information would you omit from a shareable HTML report?”
- “Where is this working record stored?”
- “What does ‘Delete local review’ remove, and what does it leave behind?”

## Debrief

Ask:

- “Where did the interface influence your attention or wording?”
- “Where did it support or conflict with your method?”
- “Did it make any alternative interpretation easier or harder to see?”
- “When did you distrust, over-trust, or ignore the AI reading?”
- “What should be removed, not only added?”
- “What would stop you from using this workflow?”
- “What finding would disappoint the product team but be important to report?”

End using the approved local debrief and withdrawal procedure.

## Facilitator help levels

Use the lowest sufficient level and record it:

1. Repeat the task without rephrasing.
2. Ask the participant to inspect the current screen.
3. Point to a region without naming the control.
4. Name the control.
5. Demonstrate the action.

Help level is diagnostic context, not a score of participant ability.
